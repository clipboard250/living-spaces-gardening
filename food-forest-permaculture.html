What it is

What is a food forest?

A food forest is an edible garden designed to work like a natural woodland. Instead of rows you replant every season, layers of fruit trees, berry shrubs, herbs, and ground cover grow together and support each other. Once it takes hold, it produces food year after year with far less work than a traditional vegetable garden. Food forest, edible landscaping, and permaculture all describe the same idea, growing food the way a healthy ecosystem already does.

Most edible gardens around Tampa Bay are raised beds. They give you a season of vegetables, then you pull them out and start over. A food forest is built to stay. It uses plants that come back on their own, feed the soil as they grow, and need less water and replanting every year that passes. One approach gives you a harvest. The other gives you a system that keeps feeding you.

We design food forests around what Florida actually gives you, the heat, the sandy soil, the heavy summer rain, and the long dry winters. Every plant is chosen because it belongs in this climate, and placed so the rain feeds it and the soil holds together. The result is a garden that works with Florida instead of against it.

What is included

What goes into your food forest.

Every food forest is designed for the specific property, but the foundation is always the same. Here is what we build into the plan.

01

Site and Soil Assessment

We read your land first, the sun, the slope, where water sits and where it runs off, and what the soil is working with. Everything in the design starts from what your property actually has.

02

Layered Planting Design

A food forest grows in layers, from tall fruit trees down to ground cover. We map each layer so plants share light, water, and root space instead of competing, the way a real woodland does.

03

Fruit and Nut Trees

The backbone of the food forest. We choose fruit and nut trees proven to produce in the Tampa Bay climate, and place them for shade, structure, and decades of harvest.

04

Edible Shrubs and Herbs

Berry bushes, perennial herbs, and edible ground cover fill in beneath the trees. These layers produce early, hold moisture in the soil, and keep the whole system productive.

05

Water and Soil Building

We shape the ground so rain soaks in where plants need it instead of running off. Mulch and ground cover feed the soil over time, so the food forest gets richer instead of poorer.

06

Establishment Plan

The first year or two matter most. You get a clear plan for what to water, prune, and watch, and the option to have us tend it with you until it can stand on its own.

How it works

The seven layers of a food forest

A food forest copies the structure of a natural woodland, where plants grow at different heights and every layer has a job. Stacking these layers is what lets a small space produce a lot of food while taking care of itself.

LAYER ONE

Canopy

The tall fruit and nut trees that anchor the garden, provide shade, and produce for decades. They set the structure everything else grows around.

LAYER TWO

Understory

Smaller fruit trees that grow in the dappled light beneath the canopy, adding more harvest without crowding the big trees.

LAYER THREE

Shrub

Berry bushes and other edible shrubs. This layer often produces first and keeps producing through the seasons.

LAYER FOUR

Herbaceous

Herbs, greens, and flowering plants that bring food to the table, attract pollinators, and support the plants around them.

LAYER FIVE

Ground Cover

Low spreading plants that shade the soil, hold in moisture, and crowd out weeds so you do less work.

LAYER SIX

Root

Edible roots and tubers growing below the surface, making use of space the other layers do not reach.

LAYER SEVEN

Vine

Climbing edibles that use the trees and structures for support, adding yet another layer of food from the same footprint.
