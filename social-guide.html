Voice rules

TL;DR
Sentence case. Short sentences. Specific over general. Never 'Kia.' Never blend with LR.
  • Never call Kiamesha 'Kia' in any caption, post, script, or comment
  • LSG and LR are separate businesses. Never blend them in the same post.
  • No em dashes anywhere in written copy
  • Do not use: elevate, thrive, craft, magical, impact, brand, noise, voice
  • Sentence case for all captions and on-screen text (not ALL CAPS except logo lockups)
  • Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
  • Specific over general: 'sunshine mimosa' beats 'native groundcover'
  • Credentials belong mid-content, not the opening line
  • Never open a video with 'Hi I'm...' or 'Today I want to talk about...' -- start in the middle of something
  • No generic inspirational quotes unconnected to project work
  • No public negative comment responses without a plan

Each platform has a practical sweet spot. Click any platform to see the full breakdown and character limits.

Video production

TL;DR
Film vertical. Edit in CapCut. Add captions and logo. Every idea can become a 6-second, 15-second, and longer version from one filming session.

Short-form video on TikTok, Reels, and YouTube Shorts should be filmed and exported in vertical format. Phone upright the entire time. A video with black bars top and bottom because it was filmed horizontally will perform poorly.

The Redington Beach video is a good example -- horizontal video in a vertical frame with black bars. Every video going forward should fill the full vertical frame.

A large portion of TikTok and Reels viewers watch with sound off. TikTok auto-generates captions but they contain errors and cannot be styled.

StepWhat to do
1. Film vertical9:16 ratio. Phone upright the entire time.
2. Edit in CapCutTrim, add captions, add logo watermark, add on-screen text.
3. Add logoConsistent corner. Small. Not covering the action.
4. Export at 1080pDo not export compressed. The platform will compress further.
5. Upload thumbnailYouTube: custom thumbnail always. TikTok: add a cover frame.

Example: a 2-minute shoreline restoration walkthrough gives you a 15-second teaser for Reels, a 6-second clip for Shorts, and the full video for YouTube. One filming session, three posts.

Open with the viewer's situation, not the company name. Bring in credentials once you have their attention.

  • Start with the viewer's problem or question, not your name
  • Credentials earn more trust mid-video, after you have already said something useful
  • Kiamesha on camera is an asset. Her voice and expertise are the brand.
  • Ether's B-roll is an asset. Real project footage beats most talking-head content.

Hook formulas

TL;DR
A hook creates a question the viewer can only answer by watching. 8 formulas, each with an LSG and LR version.

'It feels like walking on moss' earned 1.3M likes and 59K bookmarks. 'This was WAY bigger than I thought' earned 3.2M likes and 119K bookmarks. Both work because they create a question before explaining anything.

Lead with how something feels, looks, smells, or sounds.

LSG
It feels like walking on moss. (Native groundcovers replacing turf grass.)
LR
The water here was completely still three months ago. (Restored wetland.)

Start with something that appears one size and turns out to be another.

LSG
This was WAY bigger than I thought. (Root ball, invasive spread, or scale of install.)
LR
I thought this shoreline was 20 feet. It was 200.

Name the specific frustration your audience has. They stop scrolling because they feel seen.

LSG
Your Florida lawn is probably dying because of this mistake.
LR
You've watched that bank erode a little more every storm season. You don't have to keep watching it.

Lead with a concrete, measurable result. 'Native yard' is generic. 'This St. Pete yard uses 80% less water' is a fact the viewer can picture.

LSG
This St Pete yard uses 80% less water than a typical lawn.
LR
This pond restoration removed three invasive plant species in one season.

Say the thing that contradicts what most people believe.

LSG
Stop planting these five plants in Florida.
LR
Why seawalls damage Florida coastlines.

Show something real before saying anything. The image is the hook.

LSG
What a real Florida native yard looks like.
LR
Before and after: shoreline restoration in Tampa Bay.

Connect the work to something larger than the individual property.

LR
What happens when wetlands disappear.
LR
Three invasive plants destroying Florida wetlands.

Use a real credential naturally mid-observation. It should feel like a passing detail, not a pitch.

LSG
I've been a Florida Master Naturalist since 2014. Here's the mistake I still see in almost every yard.
LR
What makes a restoration contractor qualified. (FANN credential and what it actually requires.)
